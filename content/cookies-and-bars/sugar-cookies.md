# Sugar Cookies

Sugar Cookies are a good recipe to keep in a baking collection because they offer a different flavor, texture, or technique from the other treats in this category. This page includes a starting recipe, what to watch for in the oven, and small adjustments that can help you get consistent results. Oven temperatures and pan sizes matter, so check your bake as it gets close to the suggested time.

![Homemade sugar cookies decorated with colorful pink, purple, mint, and white icing and rainbow sprinkles on a plate](../images/sugar-cookie.jpg)


## Recipe at a glance

- **Yield:** About 30 cookies
- **Oven:** 350°F (175°C)
- **Bake time:** 8–10 minutes
- **Equipment:** Mixing bowls, measuring tools, parchment paper, baking pan or sheet, and a cooling rack

## Ingredients

- **1 cup (226 g)** unsalted butter, softened
- **1 cup** granulated sugar
- **1** large egg
- **2 teaspoons** vanilla extract
- **2 3/4 cups** all-purpose flour
- **1 teaspoon** baking powder
- **1/2 teaspoon** salt

## Instructions

1. Beat the butter and sugar together until creamy. Mix in the egg and vanilla.
2. In a separate bowl, combine flour, baking powder, and salt. Stir into the butter mixture to form a soft dough.
3. Divide the dough into two discs, wrap, and chill for at least one hour so it is easier to roll.
4. Heat the oven and line two baking sheets with parchment. Roll dough on a lightly floured surface to about 1/4 inch thick.
5. Cut shapes with cookie cutters, moving them to the baking sheet with a spatula.
6. Bake until the edges are just beginning to color. Cool completely before decorating.

## What makes this recipe work

Sugar cookies are useful when the appearance matters as much as the flavor. A lightly floured rolling pin helps prevent sticking, but adding too much flour can make the cookies dry. Try rolling between sheets of parchment to keep the dough manageable. Chill the cut shapes for ten minutes if they become warm while you work.

### Common problems and fixes

If your shapes spread, the dough may have been too warm. If the cookies are tough, the dough may have been worked too much after adding flour. For a simple finish, dust cooled cookies with powdered sugar or use a thin icing.

> **Baking note:** Start checking a few minutes before the listed time. Ovens vary, and the appearance of the edges and center is often a better guide than the timer alone.
